I am a Research Scientist and Leccturer at the Institute of Applied Geosciences, TU Darmstadt, working at the intersection of geomechanics, computational modeling, geophysics and structural geology. My research focuses on understanding the complex interactions between geological structures, fluid flow, stress evolution and seismicity in the Earth's crust. By integrating geological observations, geophysical observations and advanced numerical simulations, I aim to develop a better understanding of how fractured reservoirs respond to natural and anthropogenic perturbations. I develop physics-based models to investigate fluid migration, stress redistribution and seismic hazard in complex geological environments. My research contributes to the development of safe and sustainable subsurface technologies supporting the energy transition, including geothermal energy, CO₂ storage, and hydrogen storage. 

My academic journey began with a Bachelor’s degree in Petroleum Engineering at Amirkabir University of Technology, Iran (2006–2010) and a Master’s degree in Reservoir Geoscience and Engineering at IFP School, France (2010–2012). I completed my doctorate in Earth Sciences at ETH Zurich (2015–2018), where I investigated the links between fracture networks, stress heterogeneities, and injection-induced seismicity in deep geothermal reservoirs.

During my postdoctoral research in GErmany at several institutions, I expanded my research toward using coupled thermo-hydro-mechanical processes and physical understanding of induced earthaueks in subsurface reservoirs. As a Research Scientist and DFG Principal Investigator at TU Darmstadt, I am activaely engaged in teacing and research activities in the group of Engineering Geology.
